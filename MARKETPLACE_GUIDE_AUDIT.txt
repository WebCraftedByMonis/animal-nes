====================================================================
1. WHAT KIND OF MARKETPLACE THIS ACTUALLY IS
   (The guide assumes a vendor+affiliate model. Yours isn't that.)
====================================================================

[DIFFERENT] Business model
  The guide's whole structure rests on one idea: every product is
  either sold by a platform vendor OR is an affiliate link to
  someone else's site. Your site is neither - it's a three-party
  model: Company (a manufacturer/supplier who owns the product and
  stock), Partner (a vet or reseller who can buy at partner pricing
  and refers customers via a personal code), and the end Customer.
  Every product is tied to both a Company and a Partner. This is a
  real, working design - just a different shape than the guide
  assumes, so its "affiliate" chapters don't map onto your business
  at all.
  Evidence: prisma Product.companyId / Product.partnerId,
            Partner.referralCode / referredById

[DIFFERENT] Product catalog
  The guide wants normalized category_id/brand_id lookup tables.
  Yours stores category, sub-category and sub-sub-category as free
  text on the product itself, and instead of one price it has a
  3-tier price system per pack size (company price / dealer price /
  customer price) via ProductVariant - genuinely more useful for a
  B2B+B2C site than the guide's single-price model, just structured
  differently.
  Evidence: Product.category/subCategory/subsubCategory (text),
            ProductVariant.companyPrice/dealerPrice/customerPrice

[DIFFERENT] Animal listings
  The guide treats an "animal" as extra attributes hanging off a
  product row. Your site instead runs it as its own flow: a
  customer submits a SellAnimal listing (specie, breed, age, weight,
  gender, health certificate, price) with its own images/videos, it
  goes into a Pending -> Accepted/Rejected queue for admin review,
  and only then can it be added to a cart and checked out. It's not
  the guide's data shape, but it covers the same ground - structured
  animal data, media, and a workflow - through a dedicated "sell
  your animal" pipeline rather than a product attribute set.
  Evidence: SellAnimal (status: PENDING/ACCEPTED/REJECTED),
            SellAnimalImage, SellAnimalVideo, AnimalCart


====================================================================
2. ONBOARDING, ORDERS & MONEY
   (The parts the guide is strictest about)
====================================================================

[BUILT] Public, customer & admin pages
  Almost every page the guide lists exists in some form: home,
  animal category pages, product listing/detail pages, category
  pages, brand pages, cart, checkout, wishlist, orders, profile, and
  separate login/dashboards for Company and Partner. The admin
  backend actually goes well past what the guide asks for - you
  also have finance reporting, a dynamic form builder, media
  library, social auto-posting, sticky logo config, and email
  delivery logs, none of which the guide even mentions.
  Evidence: 60+ routes under src/app/dashboard,
            company/dashboard, partner/dashboard

[MISSING] Vendor / partner onboarding
  The guide wants self-service signup -> document upload -> admin
  review -> approved/rejected/suspended, plus a six-state product
  lifecycle (draft/pending/approved/rejected/suspended/archived).
  On your site, Companies and Partners are created directly by the
  admin (/dashboard/addCompany, /dashboard/addPartner) - there's no
  public signup form, no document upload step, and no
  approval-status field on either model at all. Products only have
  a plain on/off isActive flag and an isFeatured flag, not a
  lifecycle. This is a real, simpler trade-off: tighter control, no
  self-serve growth channel.
  Evidence: no status field on Company or Partner in schema,
            Product.isActive (bool), isFeatured (bool)

[MISSING] Multi-vendor checkout
  One cart can contain products from several different Companies -
  that part works. But the guide's "one master order that splits
  into a sub-order per vendor" (so each vendor only sees and
  fulfills their own slice, with its own shipping/commission/
  refund) doesn't exist. Every order is one flat Checkout with line
  items; there's no per-company grouping, so fulfillment across
  vendors has to be sorted out manually by an admin rather than
  routed automatically.
  Evidence: Checkout -> CheckoutItem (no vendor_order split)

[MISSING] Partner wallet & withdrawals
  The guide is explicit: never let a wallet be a single number you
  edit directly - keep a ledger, so every rupee/dirham has a paper
  trail. Right now Partner.walletBalance is exactly that single
  mutable number: it gets incremented on referral/premium events
  and decremented straight away when a withdrawal is approved, with
  no ledger of individual transactions behind it. If a balance ever
  looked wrong, there'd be nothing to reconcile against. The good
  news: you already have the pattern built correctly elsewhere - the
  Finance module (Transaction, Expense, PartnerDistribution) is a
  proper append-only ledger for company revenue and business-
  partner profit-shares. It just isn't the system feeding the
  partner wallet.
  Evidence: Partner.walletBalance direct increment/decrement in
            /api/admin/withdrawal-requests/route.ts;
            Finance ledger exists separately: Transaction, Expense,
            PartnerDistribution

[MISSING] Affiliate system
  Zero trace of it - no affiliate partners, links, clicks,
  conversions, commissions, or payouts anywhere in the schema or
  code. The only thing that resembles "affiliate" is the Partner
  referral code, which is a who-referred-whom credit for your own
  wallet system, not the guide's outbound-click-to-external-seller
  tracking. Since your business model isn't affiliate-based to
  begin with (see above), this may simply not apply to you - but
  it's worth confirming that's a deliberate choice, not an
  oversight.
  Evidence: 0 matches for "affiliate" across prisma + src


====================================================================
3. THE "INTELLIGENCE" LAYER
   (Tracking, ranking, recommendations, search, analytics)
====================================================================

[MISSING] Event tracking & visitor interest
  No product-impression, product-view, click, cart-add, or purchase
  event log exists anywhere. Nothing records what a visitor looked
  at or clicked, so there's no data to build interest profiles or
  personalization from later, even if you wanted to.

[MISSING] Ranking & recommendation engine
  Product order on the shop page is a fixed sort: featured items
  first, then newest first - that's it. There's no weighted
  scoring, no admin screen to tune it, and no "Because You Viewed,"
  "Trending," or "Similar Products" logic anywhere. This isn't
  wrong for a site your size, but it means every listing shows the
  same order to every visitor.
  Evidence: orderBy: [isFeatured desc, createdAt desc]

[DIFFERENT] Search
  There's no dedicated site search page or search engine (no
  Elasticsearch/OpenSearch), but the products page does have a
  genuinely solid filter bar - keyword, category, sub-category,
  company, price range, and sort - that's a real, working
  implementation of "search," just simpler and database-query-based
  rather than a relevance-ranked engine with behavioral signals.
  Evidence: ProductsClient.tsx: search, category, price, sort
            params

[MISSING] Analytics & funnel reporting
  No visitor/session/conversion-funnel dashboard exists. What you
  do have is a genuinely useful Finance dashboard (revenue,
  transactions, profit, expenses) - but that's money reporting, not
  the behavioral analytics (impressions -> views -> clicks ->
  purchases) the guide means.


====================================================================
4. SEO, PERFORMANCE & SECURITY
   (Where the site is strongest)
====================================================================

[BUILT] SEO foundations
  This is the best-matched part of the whole guide. Clean slug URLs
  (/products/cattle-feed/premium-dairy-feed, not ?id=1837), JSON-LD
  structured data on 20+ page types, a real robots.txt with per-bot
  rules, and a sitemap system that pre-generates static XML nightly
  so nginx serves it without hitting your database on every crawl -
  that last part is more sophisticated than what the guide even
  asks for. You're also redirecting www -> non-www correctly. The
  one gap: no admin screen to edit meta titles/descriptions or
  manage redirects without a code change.
  Evidence: generate-sitemaps.js (nightly cron), JSON-LD on
            product/animal/brand/vet pages, robots.txt per-bot
            rules

[DIFFERENT] Performance
  Images run through Cloudinary and next/image with WebP, and the
  homepage uses ISR (regenerates every 30 minutes instead of on
  every request) - both real wins. But Redis is installed and
  configured in package.json, and never actually imported or used
  anywhere in the code - it's a dead dependency, not a caching
  layer. There's also no background job queue; heavy work like AI
  description generation or product scraping runs as a direct
  long-running request rather than a queued job.
  Evidence: redis in package.json, 0 imports in src/;
            next.config.ts images.formats webp, minimumCacheTTL

[DIFFERENT] Security
  Passwords are hashed with bcrypt across every login type, and
  Admin, Company, Partner and Customer each get their own session
  table - a real, correct separation of roles. What's missing
  versus the guide: no rate limiting, no explicit CSRF tokens, no
  admin 2FA. Payments aren't verified by a webhook because there
  isn't a payment gateway to webhook - customers upload a screenshot
  and an admin approves it by eye. That's a legitimate, common
  approach for COD-heavy markets, but it does mean a faked
  screenshot is a real (if manual) risk worth knowing about, not a
  coding bug to fix.
  Evidence: bcrypt used in auth/register, admin-auth, company-auth,
            partner-auth; AdminSession, CompanySession,
            PartnerSession, Session (separate tables)


====================================================================
BOTTOM LINE
====================================================================

Your site is not an unfinished version of the PDF's marketplace - it
is a different, working business (a company -> partner -> customer
veterinary/livestock marketplace) that happens to share a few
chapters with the guide's affiliate/vendor blueprint. Where the
guide's basics apply - clean URLs, structured data, role-based
logins, hashed passwords - you're solidly there, and your SEO
plumbing and admin backend both go further than the guide asks for.

The genuinely open gap is the whole "intelligence" layer the guide
spends a third of its pages on: no event tracking, no ranking
weights, no recommendations, no behavioral analytics. None of that
breaks anything today, but it's also the part with no shortcut - it
has to be built from a blank page whenever you're ready for it.

The other real fix worth prioritizing sooner is turning the partner
wallet from a raw editable number into a ledger, since you already
have the right pattern built (the Finance module) - it just isn't
connected to the wallet yet.
